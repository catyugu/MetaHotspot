# Residual reconstruction and matrix-convex chord certificates

Research on `agent/experiment20261007`, starting from
`098782a0f3484bedd2e63b166dfbabaa4f93b303`. This is a proof of sufficient
conditions, not a claim that the full Case1 parameter range passes them.
Ordinary floating-point evaluation is **not** an outward-rounded certificate.
CG residual defects are included analytically; arithmetic rounding is not.

## 1. Contract and assumptions

For a fixed parameter h in a supplied rectangular box, let

\[
 C\dot x+K(h)x=Gu,\qquad x(0)=0,\quad
 K(h)=K_0+\sum_i h_iH_i.
\]

C is positive diagonal, K0 symmetric positive semidefinite conservative
M-matrix conduction, Hi are nonnegative diagonal Robin matrices, h_i>0, and
K(h) is SPD. A positive supersolution checked below establishes this last
condition for the matrices used here. HTC is constant in time. V is fixed and
C-orthonormal. Let A(h)=V^T K(h)V, B=V^TG, xhat=Vz,
zdot+A(h)z=Bu. The targets remain relative full-field steady K-energy <=epsilon
and relative full-field step C-energy <=sqrt(epsilon), before SVD, for all input
combinations. This prototype does not compress or export a production ROM.

Change input coordinates so B^TB=I; this is invertible when B has full column
rank, and does not change the all-input relative error contract. Initial slope
vectors C^-1 G are included in candidate generation. The actual residual
R0=G-CVB is nevertheless explicitly bounded rather than assumed numerically zero.

## 2. Elliptic reconstruction identity

Define D(h)=K(h)V-CVA(h), L(h)=K(h)^-1D(h), and
rho(t)=-L(h)z(t). The error generated by -Dz obeys

\[
 C\dot e+K(h)e=K(h)\rho,\quad e(0)=0.
\]

With the C-selfadjoint contraction semigroup S_h(t), integration by parts gives

\[
 e(t)=\rho(t)-\int_0^t S_h(t-s)\dot\rho(s)\,ds,
 \quad
 \|e(t)u\|_C\le\|L(h)z(t)u\|_C+
 \int_0^t\|L(h)e^{-A(h)s}Bu\|_C\,ds.                 \tag{1}
\]

The constant initial residual R0 contributes at most

\[
 \min(t\|C^{-1/2}R_0\|,\|C^{-1/2}R_0\|/\alpha_{box})\|u\|.
\]

The second assertion follows from (I-S_h(t))K(h)^-1R0 and the scalar spectral
bound ||I-S_h||_C<=1. Equation (1) retains the lifted residual directions;
it does not add the separate variations of the two large state trajectories.
There is no full-system eigenbasis in (1).

Elliptic reconstruction itself is established literature, not an invention of
this experiment: Makridakis–Nochetto (2003), and its MOR extension by
Ohlberger–Rave–Schindler (2017), listed in the experiment report.

## 3. Spectral lower bound that is not a Ritz estimate

An irreducible symmetric M-matrix has an entrywise positive lowest eigenvector
in C-scaled coordinates. For w>0 the Collatz–Wielandt inequality gives

\[
 \lambda_{min}(K_c,C)\ge
 \alpha_c=\min_j (K_cw)_j/(Cw)_j.
\]

Compute an approximate w using AMG-CG for Kc w=C1, then **use its actual
matrix product**, checking w>0 and alpha_c>0. Convergence alone is not the
certificate. For conservative connected conduction plus grounded Robin terms,
the Perron argument applies. Equivalently the positive supersolution criterion
and symmetric Z-matrix structure establish positive definiteness.

At the arithmetic box center c, positivity of K0 and Hi implies

\[
 -\rho K_c\preceq K(h)-K_c\preceq\rho K_c,
 \quad\rho=\max_i (b_i-a_i)/(2c_i)<1.
\]

Thus K(h)>=(1-rho)Kc and alpha_box=(1-rho)alpha_c.
This easily checked form radius is conservative; it is not a computed full
spectral norm or a source-dependent Ritz value.

## 4. Directional affine lift, with actual CG defects

Use one Kc AMG setup. Solve approximately

\[
 Y\approx K_c^{-1}D_c,\quad d_0=D_c-K_cY,
\]

and, for each i,

\[
 S_i=D_i-H_iY,\quad Z_i\approx K_c^{-1}S_i,
 \quad d_i=S_i-K_cZ_i.
\]

For delta=h-c, put Z(delta)=sum delta_i Zi and the retained trial
T(delta)=Y+Z(delta). Its exact lifting defect is

\[
 D(h)-K(h)T(h)=d_0+\sum_i\delta_i d_i-
            [K(h)-K_c]Z(\delta).                    \tag{2}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \|C^{1/2}(L(h)-T(h))\|\le
 {\|C^{-1/2}d_0\|+\|C^{-1/2}\sum\delta_id_i\|
       \over(1-\rho)\alpha_c}
 +{\rho\over(1-\rho)\sqrt{\alpha_c}}
       \|Z(\delta)\|_{K_c}.                         \tag{3}
\]

For the last term whiten Kc, bound the relative perturbation by rho, its
inverse by 1/(1-rho), and convert Kc-energy to C-energy with alpha_c.
Both norms depending on delta are convex; their maxima over a rectangle are
bounded at its vertices. The second term in (3) is quadratic in box width.
Let eta be the resulting uniform upper bound. This is the only scalar lifting
remainder; the affine trial is retained as a direction-dependent Gram.

Counts: one supersolution RHS, r center lift RHS and d*r innovation RHS.
Therefore the current step prototype uses 1+(d+1)r full inverse RHS per box.
This is a serious cost limitation, not free reduced computation.

## 5. Continuous parameter transfer without a full-state perturbation

Let Ac=A(c), E(h)=A(h)-Ac, beta=lambda_min(A(a)). Duhamel gives

\[
 T(h)[e^{-A(h)s}-e^{-A_cs}]
 =-\int_0^s T(h)e^{-A_cv}E(h)e^{-A(h)(s-v)}dv.
\]

Since ||B||=1 and ||exp(-A(h)v)||<=exp(-beta v), integration over s gives

\[
 \int_0^t\|L(h)e^{-A(h)s}B\|_C ds
 \le I(t)+ f_t(\beta)[\eta+J(t)],                  \tag{4}
\]

where f_t(lambda)=(1-exp(-lambda t))/lambda and uniform envelopes satisfy

\[
 I(t)\ge\sup_h\int_0^t\|C^{1/2}T(h)e^{-A_cs}B\|ds,
\]
\[
 J(t)\ge\sup_h\int_0^t\|C^{1/2}T(h)e^{-A_cs}E(h)\|ds.
\]

The same derivative comparison implies

\[
 \|L(h)z_h(t)-T(h)z_c(t)\|_C\le
              f_t(\beta)[\eta+J(t)].               \tag{5}
\]

Combining (1), (4), (5) yields the all-input absolute bound

\[
 N(t)=R(t)+I(t)+2f_t(\beta)[\eta+J(t)]
      +\min(tr_0,r_0/\alpha_{box}),
\quad R(t)\ge\sup_h\|C^{1/2}T(h)z_c(t)\|.
\]

T(h) is affine. Its derivative Gram and R(t) are bounded at parameter
vertices by norm convexity. The product T(h)exp(-Ac s)E(h) is polynomial,
degree at most two in each parameter. Its **exact tensor Bernstein controls**
are formed from the affine vertex controls: for multiindex nu, sum products
of vertex indices v,w with v+w=nu and weight product 1/binom(2,nu_i).
The operator norm of the polynomial is at most the maximum control norm.
This is continuous-domain coverage, not vertex sampling of the response.

## 6. Analytic time integrals and infinite tail

Diagonalize only Ac, an r by r matrix. For any retained lifting coefficient
and reduced input matrix F, the exact integrated observation Gram on [a,b] is

\[
 \mathcal Q_{ab}=F^T[M\circ H_{ab}]F,\quad
 (H_{ab})_{ij}=e^{-(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)a}
 {1-e^{-(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)(b-a)}\over\lambda_i+\lambda_j}.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the integral of the operator norm by
sqrt((b-a) lambda_max(Q_ab)). For the r-column Bernstein product controls,
the prototype instead uses sqrt((b-a) trace(Q_ab)), a further valid but
potentially expensive relaxation in tightness. Cross terms are retained,
including signed modal cancellations. A sum of single-mode absolute values
is only used for the exponentially small tail beyond T=35/lambda_min(Ac).

On each temporal interval [a,b], the numerator is at most
R(a)+I(a)+2[I(b)-I(a)]+2 f_b(beta)[eta+J(b)] plus the initial residual bound.
The extra integral controls the variation of R(t). This covers every time
inside the interval. The finite grid does not serve as a time sample proof.
For t>=T use the exact constant limit of the trial response, its modal tail,
I(infinity), J(infinity) and f_infinity(beta). At t near zero, bound
||e(t)||C<=t*r0+t^2*Dmax/2; z(t) has a t-exp(-bmax*t) lower bound.
Thus zero, all finite times and infinity are covered analytically.

## 7. Input-aware denominator

A worst-mode scalar f_t(lambda_max) is needlessly small for thermal sources.
Use the scalar inequality 1-exp(-x)>=x/(1+x), functional calculus, and the
monotonicity of the positive inverse:

\[
 B^T f_t(A(h))B\succeq
 B^T[A(h)+t^{-1}I]^{-1}B\succeq
 B^T[A(b)+t^{-1}I]^{-1}B.
\]

Because B has orthonormal columns, for any u,

\[
 \|z_h(t)u\|\ge
 \lambda_{min}\{t B^T[I+tA(b)]^{-1}B\}\|u\|.
\]

This rational lower bound is monotone in t and valid throughout the box.
An alternative lower bound is sigma_min(zc(a)) minus
||E|| f_b(beta) f_b(lambda_min(Ac)) on [a,b]. The maximum of these two lower
bounds is valid. The infinite tail must use the **infinite** drift bound,
not drift at T; the implementation does so.

If N(t)<=q||z_h(t)u|| for every u with q<1, reverse triangle gives

\[
 \|e(t)u\|_C/\|x(t)u\|_C\le q/(1-q).
\]

An evaluated q>=1 means **unresolved**, not large true error. A finite upper
bound above the target likewise does not reject the physical ROM by itself.

## 8. A separate cheap continuous steady certificate

Let Q(h)=G^TK(h)^-1G and Qr(h)=B^TA(h)^-1B. Galerkin orthogonality gives the
exact steady error Gram E^T K(h)E=Q(h)-Qr(h). The full inverse is operator
convex. Therefore its multilinear corner chord dominates Q(h) throughout
the box. The reduced variational trial Z=A(c)^-1B gives the affine lower bound

\[
 Q_r(h)\succeq \ell(h)=B^TZ+Z^TB-Z^TA(h)Z.
\]

At a full corner, compute X approximately and d=G-KX. The identity

\[
 Q=G^TX+X^TG-X^TKX+d^TK^{-1}d
\]

and common verified K>=alpha_low C give the computable PSD upper bound
U=G^TX+X^TG-X^TKX+alpha_low^-1 d^TC^-1d.
Thus Q(h)-Qr(h) is dominated by the multilinear interpolant of U_v-ell(h_v).
Also Q(h)>=Qr(h)>=Qr(b) by Loewner inverse monotonicity. Hence

\[
 B_s^2=\max_v\max(0,\lambda_{max}(U_v-\ell(h_v),Q_r(b)))
\]

certifies the continuous relative steady K-energy error. Its cost is
m*2^d+1 full inverse RHS: **17 for Case1's two parameters and four inputs**,
independent of r. The chord curvature is not canceled by this proof; it can
be much looser than the existing 36-RHS residual Bernstein certificate.
No claim of uniformly tighter certification is made.

## 9. Scope and remaining obstruction

These are finite-dimensional discretized-model certificates in exact
arithmetic, not PDE discretization error bounds, maximum-node guarantees,
Hankel-norm bounds, time-varying HTC guarantees, or post-SVD certificates.
No saturation assumption is used. No guarantee is inferred from richer-ROM
reference agreement. These constructions establish analytical continuous-domain
bounds on a supplied box; the supplied full Case1 box still needs to pass them.
The input-unweighted lifting remainder eta and Frobenius product-control
bound remain likely bottlenecks. Their removal, with a cheap rigorously enclosed
input-aware correction space, remains an open research task here.
